What this is

Questions lead to understanding.

Voices of Resilience is a documentary road series where each episode begins with a central question that challenges biases and perspectives. Travelling across Indigenous communities across Treaties 6, 7 & 8, we meet Elders, Knowledge Keepers, and community voices whose perspectives reshape Tyler’s understanding of resilience in a rapidly changing world.

The series is about curiosity, connection, and the adaptation of traditional Indigenous culture in an ever-changing world. It is built around universal questions and human-centred storytelling — a format designed to travel far beyond Alberta.

The Why

Why we're doing this

Stories live on the land. We're meeting them there.

Audiences today are hungry for authenticity and adventure. They want real people asking meaningful questions in a time of filters and performances. They're drawn to diverse cultural stories, nomadic storytelling (VanLife!), and exploration of new lands and perspectives.

Voices of Resilience sits at the crossroads of it all. This show speaks to audiences searching foridentity, community, direction, and growth. It offers a journey where vulnerability is celebrated and wisdom transcends cultural biases.

Story sovereignty
Communities define themselves. Participants are collaborators, not subjects. Ongoing consent. Uncut recordings returned.
Relationship first
Every story begins long before a camera is introduced. Elders and Knowledge Keepers guide the process from day one.
Non-extractive
VoR is built in direct opposition to transactional documentary practices. Community is not a backdrop. Culture is not a checkbox.
Built to travel
Central Alberta is the starting point. Treaties 6, 7, and 8 are next. Then all of Canada. Then the world.

Who's behind this

A learner willing to grow out loud.

Tyler McKinney is Cree, Métis, Vietnamese, and Chinese. He is also Two-Spirit. Culture was never passed down to him — and Voices of Resilience began as a direct response to that journey. A space to hold questions he didn't grow up with answers to.

With over a decade of photography and videography experience, two funded seasons through TELUS STORYHIVE, and three years facilitating the I Am a Kind Man initiative with Indigenous men and youth, Tyler brings humility and a willingness to learn through listening.

Produced by Guillermo Barraza through Nomadic Wildling Media, with guidance from Elders and Knowledge Keepers across Treaties 6, 7, and 8.
